MCCALLIE SCHOOL STRENGTH & CONDITIONING · SUMMER 2026

Jump Performance & Force Analysis

Turning years of athlete performance data into statistical benchmarks coaches can use to better understand jump performance and force production.

R Statistical Analysis Regression Data Cleaning Data Visualization Performance Analytics

01 · THE PROBLEM

Turning testing data into something coaches could use.

McCallie School had several years of countermovement jump testing data. The goal of my project was to analyze this information and better understand the relationship between jump height and relative propulsive force.

Instead of simply summarizing the data, I wanted to develop benchmarks that could help coaches understand whether an athlete was producing a typical amount of force for the height they were jumping.

02 · THE DATA

Countermovement jump data from 2023–2026.

I analyzed McCallie's countermovement jump data collected between 2023 and 2026. The primary variables used in the analysis were jump height and average relative propulsive force as a percentage of bodyweight.

04 · NORMATIVE ANALYSIS

What is normal for each jump height?

I grouped athletes based on jump height and calculated normative relative-force ranges for each group. The 25th and 75th percentiles were used to define the typical range of force production.

This created a baseline that allows an athlete's force production to be compared with other jumps in the same performance range.

06 · RELATIONSHIP ANALYSIS

Does producing more relative force lead to higher jumps?

I used correlation and linear regression to analyze the overall relationship between average relative propulsive force and jump height.

The analysis showed a positive relationship between relative propulsive force and jump height. Athletes producing more force relative to their bodyweight generally jumped higher.

The model produced an R² of approximately 0.475, meaning relative propulsive force explained about 47.5% of the variation in jump height within the data.

07 · KEY FINDING

Force matters, but it doesn't explain everything.

Relative propulsive force had a clear positive relationship with jump height, but it explained only part of the variation in overall jump performance.

This was important because it showed that relative force is useful for evaluating performance while also demonstrating that other factors contribute to how high an athlete ultimately jumps.

08 · IMPACT

Turning analysis into a coaching tool.

The final result was more than a statistical report. I developed benchmarks and visualizations that made it easier to compare an individual athlete's force production with the broader McCallie population.

The analysis gave the strength and conditioning staff a clearer way to interpret jump-testing results and use the data when evaluating athlete performance.

09 · WHAT I LEARNED

Analysis has to be understandable to be useful.

This project gave me experience taking a real-world dataset from analysis through final communication. I strengthened my skills in R, statistical analysis, regression, data visualization, and translating technical results for a nontechnical audience.

Most importantly, I learned that finding a statistical result is only part of the process. The result becomes valuable when it can be clearly communicated and used to support better decisions.
